# Quote, passage, image, or claim provenance

Treat remembered wording and attribution as hypotheses.

1. Search one or two short, distinctive fragments rather than a long quotation. Favor
   unusual words, numbers, names, and wording likely to survive paraphrase.
2. Break a long passage into beginning, middle, and ending fragments. Search quoted and
   unquoted versions, then vary punctuation, spelling, morphology, and word order.
3. Search the alleged speaker/author separately from the wording. Try attribution verbs,
   approximate dates, venues, interviews, books, transcripts, captions, or surrounding
   subject matter.
4. Search paraphrases and progressively longer, less polished variants. Retellings often
   become shorter, smoother, and more memorable; an awkward expanded version may be
   closer to the source.
5. Open works that quote the passage and inspect their footnotes, bibliographies, nearby
   first mention, and earlier editions. A repeated citation is not independent evidence
   if every instance descends from the same unsourced intermediary.
6. For images, reverse-search when available and also search captions, filenames,
   credits, visible text, event details, and cropped/uncropped variants.
7. Establish the earliest verifiable appearance. Distinguish exact quote, translation,
   paraphrase, later attribution, and no-source folklore.

Report exact wording only from a source you opened. Keep quotations brief and within
copyright limits; otherwise paraphrase and cite.
